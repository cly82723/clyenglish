## Opener and closing drafts

### Read the task
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can find every job in a Part A task and use every note.
- Open: Which uses the note better?
  - A (Level 3): Drop books before 13 March.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Please drop your books at Room 204 before 13 March.
  - Reveal [NEW]: The second says where, and it reads as a sentence, not a copied note.
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): Please drop your books at Room 204 before 13 March.
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: Drop your books at Room 204 by 13 March, and each one earns you a swap token for the fair.
  - Reveal: Rephrase every note and add one small detail. A copied note reads like a list. (CLY's line, from the page.)

### Formats
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can open and close each Part A text type correctly.
- Open: Which opening and closing fit the book-fair email?
  - A (Level 3): Dear all students, … Yours faithfully, Chris
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Dear fellow students, … Best regards, Chris Wong
  - Reveal [NEW]: "Dear all students" is the one to avoid, and "Yours faithfully" is for a letter that began "Dear Sir or Madam". (Both rules are on the page.)
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): Dear fellow students, … Best regards, Chris Wong
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: Dear fellow students, … Best regards, Chris Wong, Chairperson, English Club
  - Reveal: Your name and your role from the task, every time. Format is the easiest Organisation mark. (CLY's line, from the page.)

### Plan
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can plan a Part A answer in five minutes.
- Open: Which student protects the Content mark better?
  - A (Level 3): Starts writing at once and tries to remember the notes.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Numbers the notes and groups them into three paragraphs.
  - Reveal [NEW]: Numbering the notes means none can be forgotten. (From the page: it is the simplest way to protect your Content mark.)
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): Numbers the notes and groups them into three paragraphs.
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: Numbers the notes, groups them, and plans one extra detail for each paragraph.
  - Reveal [NEW]: The extra detail is what stops the answer reading like a copied list.
- Note: these four lines come from the "How each level plans" aside I removed in the trim, because on its own it described a habit. Here they are used as the two answers to one question, which is what they are.

### Opening
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can open a Part A answer so that both purposes, donate and attend, show in the first paragraph.
- Open: Which opening works better?
  - A (Level 3): I am Chris Wong. I am writing to tell you about the book fair. It will be held on 15 March in the school hall. Please come.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Have you got books gathering dust on your shelf? The English Club needs them! On Friday 15 March, we're holding Swap, Don't Shop, a book fair where every book you donate earns you a token to take another one home.
  - Reveal: A is complete but choppy, and "Please come" gives no reason to come. B hooks the reader and both purposes appear in the first paragraph. (From the page.)
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): the Level 5 paragraph above
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: Every bookshelf has one: the novel you finished in Form 2 and never opened again. On Friday 15 March, the English Club wants to give it a second life. Bring it to Swap, Don't Shop, trade it for something new to you, and help a local charity at the same time.
  - Reveal: The reader recognises themselves in the first line, and all three benefits arrive in three sentences. (From the page.)
