## Step 1 · Choose and plan

Open question (Choosing): Two students are choosing a question. Who is likelier to score higher?
- A: "I like sport, so I'll do the sports question."
- B ✔: "I'll take the proposal: I have three points, and I know its format by heart."
- Reveal [NEW]: Most students pick A. Content marks reward ideas you can develop, not topics you like. Three points you can explain fill about 400 words.
- Goal line [NEW]: By the end you can choose a question in three minutes and plan it in ten.

Open question (Planning): Which plan is stronger?
- A: Intro · point 1 · point 2 · point 3 · conclusion
- B ✔: Three ideas named: learning, health, the other side.
- Reveal [NEW]: A plan that would fit any topic is not a plan yet. B names the ideas.

Closing question: Which is the better reason to cross out Q5 (the vegan blog post)?
- A [NEW]: It is not an exciting topic.
- B ✔: One point, and every detail would be invented.

## Step 2 · Content

Open question: Which idea is developed better?
- A: Opening facilities is good for health.
- B ✔: Opening facilities is good for health because many residents in crowded districts have nowhere to exercise after work.
- Reveal: B answers "why". (Existing note: "'Why' is answered. 'How' and 'so what' are still missing.")
- Goal line [NEW]: By the end you can turn one idea into why, how and so what, with a Hong Kong example.

Closing question: Which is the stronger version?
- A: Opening facilities is good for health because many residents in crowded districts have nowhere to exercise after work.
- B ✔: In districts like Sham Shui Po, where many families live in small flats and public courts are often full, a school basketball court open until nine would be the only free space many residents have to exercise. A city that keeps its courts locked at night is paying for its people's poor health twice: once to build the courts, and again in hospital bills.
- Reveal: Why (no space), how (a court open until nine), so what (the city pays twice), and a real district the reader can picture.

## Step 3 · Organisation

Open question: Which last sentence says why the paragraph matters?
- A: So schools should start later.
- B ✔: As a result, they arrive tired and learn less in their first lessons.
- Reveal [NEW]: A repeats the first line, so the marker learns nothing new. B is the Impact: what happens because of the point.
- Goal line [NEW]: By the end you can build a body paragraph in four moves: Claim, Reason, Evidence, Impact.

Closing question: Which ending is stronger?
- A: As a result, they arrive tired and learn less in their first lessons.
- B ✔: If the first two lessons of every day are lost to fatigue, we are paying full price for an education students only half receive.
- Reveal: Level 5 stops at "learn less", which is true of any tired student anywhere. Level 5** turns tiredness into a cost everyone cares about. (Existing "why" note, shortened.)

## Step 4 · Language

Open question: Which sentence tells the reader more?
- A: Mobile phones are bad for students.
- B ✔: Mobile phones distract students in class.
- Reveal: "Bad how?" The reader has to guess in A. "Distract" answers it in one word.
- Goal line [NEW]: By the end you can swap a vague word for the exact one.

Closing question: Which is stronger?
- A: Mobile phones distract students in class.
- B ✔: A phone buzzing under the desk can pull a student out of a lesson three or four times an hour.
- Reveal: The reader can see it, and can check the number against their own school day.

## Step 5 · Put it together

Open question: You have five minutes left. Which fix matters more for your mark?
- A: Changing "Many student is tired" to "Many students are tired".
- B ✔: Adding two sentences that answer the part of the question no paragraph answers ("suggest one change").
- Reveal: A missing part limits your Content mark more than any weak sentence, and two sentences fix it. (Existing "why" note.)
- Goal line [NEW]: By the end you know what to check in the last five minutes, in order of marks.
