## Opener and closing drafts

### Openings
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can open with a scene, question or claim instead of "Nowadays".
- Open: Which opening makes the reader want to go on?
  - A (Level 3): Nowadays, many students use phones in class.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: When did you last sit through a lesson without checking your phone?
  - Reveal [NEW]: A could open an essay about anything. B puts the reader inside the lesson.
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): When did you last sit through a lesson without checking your phone?
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: The MTR doors slide open at Mong Kok, and two hundred people check their phones at once.
  - Reveal [NEW]: B shows a scene the reader can see, instead of asking them to imagine one.

### Linking
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can show how two ideas are related, not just that one follows another.
- Open: Which links the ideas better?
  - A (Level 3): Firstly, students are tired. Secondly, they cannot focus.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Many students are tired; as a result, they cannot focus in class.
  - Reveal: A is a list. B says tiredness causes the lack of focus. (From the page: "Firstly, secondly, lastly" shows you have a list. Better linking shows how the ideas are related.)
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): Many students are tired; as a result, they cannot focus in class.
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: Many students sleep less than six hours a night. This lack of sleep shows in their concentration.
  - Reveal: B picks up a word from the last sentence, so the link is already made. (From the page.)

### Exact words
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can swap a vague word for a word that says exactly what you mean.
- Open: Which sentence says more?
  - A (Level 3): Sleep is very important for students.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Sleep is essential for memory.
  - Reveal [NEW]: B says what sleep is important for. This page gives you swaps for good, bad, very important and more.
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): Sleep is essential for memory.
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: Without enough sleep, the brain cannot store what it learned that day.
  - Reveal: The best word is often a more exact idea, not a longer word. (CLY's line from the page.)

### Sentence variety
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can mix long and short sentences on purpose.
- Open: Which reads better?
  - A (Level 3): The school has a new rule. Students cannot use phones. Many students are unhappy about it.
  - B (Level 5) ✔: Since the school banned phones last month, many students have been unhappy, arguing that they need them to check homework updates.
  - Reveal: A is three sentences of the same shape, correct but flat. B connects cause, result and reason in one. (From the page.)
- Close: Which is stronger?
  - A (Level 5): Since the school banned phones last month, many students have been unhappy, arguing that they need them to check homework updates.
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: The phone ban arrived quietly, in a one-line notice on the school website. The reaction was anything but quiet.
  - Reveal: A long sentence sets the scene and a short one lands the punch. (From the page.)

### Topic words (opener only)
- Goal [NEW]: By the end you can use a topic word with the words that usually go with it.
- Open: Which phrase sounds more like a strong script?
  - A (Level 3): give students skills
  - B (Level 5**) ✔: equip students with transferable skills
  - Reveal [NEW]: B uses *equip* with its usual partners: someone, *with*, skills. Every word on this page is shown with its partners.
