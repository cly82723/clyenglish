# Emphasis rules: one thing to look at

Written 2026-11-20 after an audit of every page. Weaker students cannot pick out what matters when many things look important at once.

## What the audit found

- Most bold on the site is purposeful: formulas, the target word in an example, list lead-ins, CREI and level labels. It is not random.
- What hurts is **stacking**: on one screen a student can meet a bordered question, a filled goal box, a highlighted sentence, bold list leads, coloured chips and small blue capitals, all asking for attention.
- Hotspots, by share of words in bold or highlight: the topic word lists (25%), Text types (128 bold spans and 19 highlights in 4,800 words), the S.3 and S.4 lessons (about 60 heavy elements in a 360-word lesson), and the first screen of a lesson page (opener, goal box and highlighted lead together).
- About 96 single words were bolded in the middle of an ordinary sentence, half of them in the "See them at work" model paragraphs, where the bold shows the taught phrase.

## The rule

1. **One focus per section.** The highlighter (`mark.swipe`) marks the single sentence to remember in a section. Never in a list, a table or an example, and not on a sentence that repeats the heading.
2. **Bold is a scan aid, and only for these.** A list lead-in or heading, a row label, the headword, and the target word or phrase inside an example. Not a whole explanatory sentence, and not words inside text that already has a bold lead-in.
3. **Colour and badges are for codes.** Levels (3, 5, 5**) and the CREI moves. Nothing else gets a chip.
4. **Everything else is plain.** A goal line, partners of a word, a "3 to 5**" side note and a checklist item are quiet text.
5. **Weight steps down, it does not repeat.** If a headword is bold, its partners are regular. If a label is bold, its value is semi-bold at most.

## What was changed

- Word lists: the partners line and the "3 to 5**" note are regular weight; the example's phrase keeps its tint with lighter bold. Bold elements per entry: 8, 3, 9, 3, 3 became 4, 2, 4, 2, 2.
- The "By the end you can" goal is a quiet line, no longer a filled box under the opener.
- On a phone, the row labels in stacked tables and the values in the Reader, Register and Format boxes are semi-bold, so the formula or the fact is what stands out.
- The "From a real Level 5 script" note is outlined and grey, not a filled orange pill, so it no longer out-shouts the word.
- The six text-type checklists and the 37 "Another topic" labels no longer bold anything.

## Not changed (CLY's decision)

- The "See them at work" paragraphs still bold every taught phrase (up to six in one paragraph).
- The coloured CREI chips on every sentence of the model letter.
- How long the S.3 and S.4 lessons are. They are mostly purposeful emphasis, but five screens tall; the S.3 openers already put one check and the quiz first, with the rules behind a tap.
