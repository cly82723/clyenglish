## Page 1: Read the three task lines first

- Goal: By the end you can say, for each task, the text type, the marks, the length, which sources it may use, and about how many content points it holds.
- Open: Which student protects the marks better?
  - A (weaker): Starts Task 8 straight away and keeps the notes open for all three tasks.
  - B (stronger) ✔: Reads all three task lines first and marks which ones say "and your notes".
  - Reveal: The task line tells you what to use. In 2022, Task 9 named the Data File only; in 2024 all three tasks named the Data File and your notes. Check every year.
- Lesson, from the official task lines [HKEAA]:

| Year | Task | Text type | Marks | Words | Sources named |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 8 | Incident report | 17 | around 140 | Data File and your notes |
| 2024 | 9 | Follow-up email | 18 | around 180 | Data File and your notes |
| 2024 | 10 | Script for a YouTube advertisement | 18 | around 180 | Data File and your notes |
| 2022 | 8 | Company history for a website | 18 | around 150 | Data File and your notes |
| 2022 | 9 | Email response | 17 | around 170 | Data File only |
| 2022 | 10 | Speech (introduction given) | 18 | around 180 | Data File and your notes |

  - **Marks tell you how many points to find.** The 2024 marking scheme gives Task Completion as 8 marks for Task 8 (17 marks) and 9 marks each for Tasks 9 and 10 (18 marks), one mark per content point: 8, 9 and 9 points. The remaining marks go to Language, Coherence and Organisation, and Appropriateness. A tutor's analysis of published exemplars (DSE Treasure) reports the same pattern since 2017: 17 marks about 8 points, 18 about 9, 19 about 10, 22 about 13.
  - **Spend time by marks.** The three tasks are close in weight, so none is a "quick one".
  - **Word counts (kept).** The same analysis found Level 5+ exemplars were on average about 1.8 times the stated length, so a text that is somewhat longer is not an automatic failure, and cutting a needed point to stay near the number is the bigger risk. Its sample is small (four published exemplars a year, three from one candidate), so treat it as a hint: cover every point first, keep each point short, and ask your teacher how your school marks length.
- Try it: For each task line above, tap whether the notes may be used. Then: "Task 9 in 2024 is worth 18 marks. How many content points should you find?" (9).
- Close: Which is stronger? A: "Task 9 in 2022 didn't mention notes, but they might help, so I'll use them." B ✔: "Task 9 in 2022 names only the Data File, so I use only the Data File." Reveal: Use the sources the line names, and nothing it doesn't.

## Page 2: Read the Data File once, and tag it

The step-by-step page. Two halves: before and after the recording.

- Goal: By the end you can turn the five minutes into a plan, and turn your notes into three ready-to-write outlines.
- Open: Which student prepares better?
  - A (weaker): Reads the whole Data File three times, once for each task.
  - B (stronger) ✔: Reads each document once and tags the task it feeds.
  - Reveal: Five minutes is not enough to read everything three times. One careful read with tags gives you the same information and leaves time to plan.
- Lesson: **Before the recording (the five minutes)**
  1. **Task lines.** Read the three lines and mark which ones use your notes (Page 1).
  2. **Situation box.** Find who you are, who your boss is, what you will hear, and where the note sheet is. In 2024: Nico Lin, secretary to the manager Ms Avery Goh at World City Travel, listening to a Zoom meeting and noting on page 3. Write your name and job title: you may need them to sign off. [HKEAA 2024]
  3. **The boss's email.** This is the instruction page. Underline every instruction and number it. In 2024: Task 8, an internal incident report for the catamaran accident, starting from the guidelines attached from the company handbook; Task 9, a follow-up email to the cancelled cruise's participants, starting with Shane Lee's email; Task 10, a fun and exciting script for a YouTube advertisement for the Adventure Fishing Tour that explains how the new luxury yacht improves the customer experience, using the Sydney Times article too. Notice the handbook is a second instruction page: it tells you what an incident report must contain.
  4. **Tag each document with a task.** One symbol per document (a circled 8, 9 or 10, or three highlighter colours). Some documents feed two tasks, so tag the part, not the page.
  5. **Read each document once.** Tick the instruction it answers; strike out what no task asks for.
  6. **Look at the note-sheet headings.** They tell you what the recording will cover, and they are shared with Part B1. In 2024 the headings were Guided Nature Walk, Bird Watching Tour, Sydney Whale Watching Cruise (the catamaran accident: findings of the Review Board, and compensation), Insects and Reptiles Exhibition, and Adventure Fishing Tour. The accident and the fishing tour belong to your tasks; strike out the rest. [HKEAA 2024]
  - **After the recording**
  7. **Match notes to points.** For each task, list its points (step 3) and next to each write where it comes from: Data File, notes, or both. A point with no source means go back to your notes now, not while writing.
  8. **Plan each task in about a minute:** opening, body points in order, closing. Use the text type's format (see Text types, and Part A: Formats for emails).
  9. **Check names, titles and tone** before you write.
  - **2024 worked example: where the points live.** The marking scheme lists, for each content point, the Data File pages it appears on, and marks the points that come only from the tapescript (the notes page) with an asterisk. In 2024, Task 8's eight points come from pages 3*, 5, 6, 7 and 10; Task 9's nine from 3*, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11; Task 10's nine from 3*, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11. Three lessons: most facts appear in more than one document, so check them against each other; some facts exist only in the recording; and a task's points are spread over many pages, so tag first. ## Page 3: Take notes while you listen

- Goal: By the end you can note a speaker's facts, reasons and opinions in a few marks, tagged by task.
- Open: Which note will help you write fastest?
  - A (weaker): A full sentence copied from the speaker.
  - B (stronger) ✔: A few words with a symbol: "27 Mar 5.50pm, Sydney Harbour; crashed on rocks ∵ weather".
  - Reveal: The speaker will not wait. Keep only what you cannot get from the Data File: numbers, names, reasons, opinions.
- Lesson:
  - **What to note:** under the printed headings, only (1) numbers, dates, names; (2) reasons and results; (3) what each speaker thinks. Skip anything already in the Data File. Leave space.
  - **Tag it:** put the task number beside a note when you hear which task it serves.
  - **Strike out** the headings you do not need (in 2024, most of the sheet) so your eye finds the right ones.
  - **Symbols**

| Mark | Means | Mark | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| → | leads to, so | ← | because of |
| ↑ ↓ | rises, falls | = | the same as |
| ≠ | different from | + | good point |
| − | bad point | ✓ | confirmed |
| ? | not sure, check later | ! | strong opinion, important |
| ∴ | so | ∵ | because |
| @ | at | & | and |

  - **Short forms**

| Short form | Means | Short form | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| w/ | with | w/o | without |
| b/c | because | esp. | especially |
| info | information | ppl | people |
| govt | government | co. | company |
| mgmt | management | dept | department |
| yr, yrs | year, years | # | number |
| % | per cent | 1st, 2nd | first, second |
| e.g. | for example | etc. | and so on |

  - **Rule:** make your own marks only if you will remember them under pressure. Practise the ones above until they are automatic, and do not invent new ones in the exam.
- Try it: The first minutes of the official 2024 Zoom meeting, from the tapescript, on the real note-sheet headings. A "Hear it" button reads the passage aloud with the browser's built-in voice. The student taps the best note for each line, then fills three gaps in a note page.
- Close: Which is stronger? A: "The catamaran crashed into rocks because the weather suddenly changed." B ✔: "Cat. → rocks ∵ sudden weather change." Reveal: Same facts, a third of the writing time.

## Page 4: Summarise charts and tables

- Goal: By the end you can describe a chart's trend in one sentence and a table's pattern with a quantity word.
- Open: Which sentence summarises the chart better? [HKEAA 2022 Performance Report]
  - A (weaker): "In 1990 there were 14 staff and in 2022 there were 65 staff."
  - B (stronger) ✔: "The number of staff has more than quadrupled since 1990."
  - Reveal: B says what the numbers mean. A only repeats two of them.
- Lesson (all figures checked against the 2022 report):
  - **Say the shape, then the size.** Staff: 14 → 65, "more than quadrupled". Profit: 20 → 214 million, "rose more than tenfold". Rankings: not in Asia's top five in 1990, second in 2022, "climbed from outside the top five to second place".
  - **Use a number only when it is the point** (an exact date, a price, a speed). A trend needs a verb, not a list of figures. (The 2024 content points do ask for exact facts such as a date or "65 knots", so know which is which.)
  - **A scale for percentages.** about 100% all · over 90% nearly all · about 80% the vast majority · about 70% most · just over half more than half · 50% half · about 40% a large minority · about 30% a minority · about 20% a small minority · about 10% few · 0% none.
  - **A table row works the same way.** The 2022 survey: 93% chose "their warm glow", 2% "old-fashioned": "the vast majority love neon's warm glow, and hardly any call it old-fashioned".
  - **Link the data to the task.** The speech was about the lasting appeal of neon, so the survey is evidence, not decoration.
- Try it: The 2022 report and survey, plus the 2024 booking calendar (reading which weeks are free), each with three sentences to choose from.
- Close: Which is stronger? A: "93% of customers loved the warm glow." B ✔: "The vast majority of customers love the warm glow." Reveal: In a text for a reader, a quantity word reads more naturally than a percentage, and the number stays in the Data File.
- Next: Page 5.

## Page 5: Use the source in your own words

- Goal: By the end you can keep the facts and change the words and the tone.
- Open: Which uses the source better? [HKEAA 2022 EPD guideline line]
  - A (weaker): "Light emitted must not exceed levels which interfere with human sleep."
  - B (stronger) ✔: "Our displays follow the EPD guidelines, so they will not disturb anyone's sleep."
  - Reveal: A copies the line and ignores the reader. B uses the same fact, in your own words, for the person you are writing to.
- Lesson:
  - **Three moves:** swap a word for a close one (interfere with → disturb), change the shape (a rule → a promise), and join it to what the reader asked about.
  - **Keep** names, numbers, dates and technical terms exactly (a date, a speed, a product name).
  - **Change the tone too.** In 2022 the minutes tell the writer to avoid blaming language. Showing care and facts is the stronger reply to worried residents than saying whose fault it is.
  - **Check each point** against step 7 on Page 2: every point answered, every point in your own words.
- Try it: Four source lines (two from 2022, two from 2024); the student taps the best rewrite for each, then spots the copied phrase in a fifth.
- Close: Which is stronger? A: "Mr Khatoon said it was not our fault." B ✔: "The law requires all outdoor displays to be dimmed by 11 pm, and our team now works with each building's management on regular maintenance." Reveal: B gives the residents facts and action, and does not argue.
