# S.3 Grammar openers (proposal for CLY's review, not built)

Every sentence below is copied from the "mistakes" list of that topic's lesson in `assets/js/data/s3-grammar-data.js` (wrong sentence, right sentence, why). The reveal is that mistake's own "why". The "one check" line is the lesson's own "tip". So nothing here is new model writing except the question stems and the plain names, marked [NEW].

Same rules as Part B: a wrong tap costs nothing, the order is shuffled, and the reveal explains either way. The tags read "Common mistake" and "Correct", the verdict words are "right and wrong" (as on Final lap), not "stronger".

## Why S.3 is different from Part B

Part B students are strong writers who read. An S.3 student with weak English will not read a long rules lesson. So the pattern is "do something, see why, do the quiz", with the rules there for whoever wants them. I have two ways to build it; please pick one.

- **A. Same as Part B.** Each topic row opens on its opener. Answering (or skipping) reveals the rules lesson, then the quiz, then one closing question. Least change; the long rules still come before the quiz.
- **B. Quiz first (my recommendation for S.3).** Opener, then the one check, then the quiz straight away. The rules lesson sits behind a tap, "See the rules", for anyone who wants them. Then the closing question. A lazy student reaches a quiz in two taps, and nothing is removed.

Either way the S.3 page stays one page of rows, and the three consolidation rows, Tenses and Practice cloze are untouched (they are exercises already).

## The eleven topics

For each: **Opener** (tap the correct sentence), the **reveal**, the **one check**, the **closing question** (a different mistake from the same topic), and a **plain name** to show under the formal one [NEW].

### 1. Indefinite pronouns · plain name: "someone, anything, nobody"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: I didn't see nobody at the bus stop.
  - Correct ✔: I didn't see anybody at the bus stop.
  - Reveal: Only one negative: "didn't" is already negative, so use "anybody".
- One check: Check the verb after every -one, -body and -thing word: it must be singular. Then count the negatives: there should be only one.
- Closing: Everyone in my class have a phone. / Everyone in my class has a phone. ✔ (Reveal: "Everyone" takes a singular verb.)

### 2. Defining and non-defining noun phrases · plain name: "extra information about a noun"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: Sushi a famous Japanese dish is easy to find.
  - Correct ✔: Sushi, a famous Japanese dish, is easy to find.
  - Reveal: It is extra information, so it needs a comma on both sides.
- One check: Cover the phrase with your finger. If you still know exactly who or what the sentence means, use commas. If not, use no commas.
- Closing: Former US President won many people's hearts. / Former US President Barack Obama won many people's hearts. ✔ (Reveal: Which president? The name is needed here, so it can't be left out.)

### 3. Bare infinitives · plain name: "let me go, made me tidy (no 'to')"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: Please let me to help you.
  - Correct ✔: Please let me help you.
  - Reveal: "Let" + someone + V1, with no "to".
- One check: Find can, must, should, had better, would rather, make or let. Then check the next verb (or the verb after the person): it must be plain V1.
- Closing: I would rather stay at home than going out. / I would rather stay at home than go out. ✔ (Reveal: Both verbs are V1: would rather + V1 + than + V1.)

### 4. So, so that and so as to · plain name: "saying why you do something"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: I moved closer so to hear the teacher.
  - Correct ✔: I moved closer so as to hear the teacher.
  - Reveal: Don't drop "as": the phrase is "so as to".
- One check: Ask: did it happen, or was it someone's plan? Happened: so. Plan: look after the gap. A subject needs "so that"; a verb alone needs "so as to".
- Closing: I whispered so as to not to wake the baby. / I whispered so as not to wake the baby. ✔ (Reveal: "Not" goes before "to", and "to" appears only once.)

### 5. Must, have to and need to · plain name: "rules and choices"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: He must to focus on his work.
  - Correct ✔: He must focus on his work.
  - Reveal: After "must", use V1 with no "to".
- One check: When you see "must not", ask: is this really forbidden? If the person is simply free to choose, you need "don't have to" or "don't need to".
- Closing: You must not come tomorrow if you are busy. / You don't have to come tomorrow if you are busy. ✔ (Reveal: It is the person's choice, not a ban, so use "don't have to".)

### 6. Past perfect · plain name: "the thing that happened first"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: The bus already left when we reached the stop.
  - Correct ✔: The bus had already left when we reached the stop.
  - Reveal: The bus left first, so it takes had + V3.
- One check: Look for two past events and ask which came first. Only that one gets "had". If there is only one past event, "had" is a mistake.
- Closing: I had gone to Ocean Park yesterday. / I went to Ocean Park yesterday. ✔ (Reveal: There is no earlier past event, so a single finished action takes the past simple.)

### 7. Present participle phrases · plain name: "Walking home, I saw…"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: Don't knowing the answer, he kept quiet.
  - Correct ✔: Not knowing the answer, he kept quiet.
  - Reveal: Make a present participle phrase negative with "not", never "don't".
- One check: After a present participle phrase, look at the first noun after the comma and ask: can this do the V-ing action? If not, the sentence is broken.
- Closing: Looking out of the window, the mountains were beautiful. / Looking out of the window, I saw how beautiful the mountains were. ✔ (Reveal: The mountains weren't looking out of the window. The person doing the looking must come after the comma.)

### 8. Past participle phrases · plain name: "Excited about the trip, we…"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: Exciting about the trip, the children couldn't sleep.
  - Correct ✔: Excited about the trip, the children couldn't sleep.
  - Reveal: The children felt excited. The person with the feeling takes -ed.
- One check: When a sentence starts with V-ing or V3, check the subject after the comma. Is it doing the action (V-ing) or receiving it (V3)? Is it the right subject at all?
- Closing: Being encouraged by his coach, so Tom entered the race. / Encouraged by his coach, Tom entered the race. ✔ (Reveal: Drop "so": the phrase already gives the reason. "Being" isn't needed either.)

### 9. Reported statements · plain name: "telling what someone said"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: He said me that he was busy.
  - Correct ✔: He told me that he was busy.
  - Reveal: "Say" can't take a person straight after it. Use "told me" or "said to me".
- One check: After "said" or "told", check three things: the tense, the pronoun and the time word. One of them is usually the trap.
- Closing: Mia said that 'I am hungry'. / Mia said that she was hungry. ✔ (Reveal: No quotation marks in reported speech, and "I" becomes "she".)

### 10. Reported questions · plain name: "telling what someone asked"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: She asked me where did I live.
  - Correct ✔: She asked me where I lived.
  - Reveal: Drop "did" and put the subject before the verb.
- One check: After "asked", "wondered" or "Do you know", read what follows. If the verb comes before the subject, swap them.
- Closing: Do you know where is the post office? / Do you know where the post office is? ✔ (Reveal: Inside a bigger question, use statement order: subject, then verb.)

### 11. Linking words · plain name: "although, because, in case"
- Opener: Which sentence is correct?
  - Common mistake: Although I was tired, but I finished my homework.
  - Correct ✔: Although I was tired, I finished my homework.
  - Reveal: Use one contrast word. "Although" already links the two parts.
- One check: For every linking word, check two things: does its job match the ideas (add, contrast, cause …)? And does the right grammar follow it (a noun or a clause, a new sentence or the same one)?
- Closing: I'll bring a jacket in case it will be cold. / I'll bring a jacket in case it is cold. ✔ (Reveal: No "will" after "in case", just like after "if".)
