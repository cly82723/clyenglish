# S.4 grammar and Text types opener drafts (for CLY's review, not built)

Same shape as the S.3 and Part B openers (see s3-openers-plan.md and part-b-opener-drafts.md): one question before the lesson ("Before you read"), one after ("Check yourself"), two choices each, a one-line reveal. A wrong tap costs nothing. A and B are shuffled on screen; the better one is marked ✔ here. Every sentence below is [NEW] and written for these drafts, so please check the wording and the examples. The "I know this. Show me the page." skip link stays on every opener.

## S.4 grammar

| Page | Slot | Question | A | B | Reveal [NEW] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tenses | Before | Which sentence is correct? | By the time we arrived, the film already started. | By the time we arrived, the film had already started. ✔ | Two past events: the earlier one takes "had" plus V3. |
| Tenses | After | Which sentence is correct? | I live here since 2022. | I have lived here since 2022. ✔ | "Since" points at a start in the past and runs to now, so use the present perfect. |
| Passive voice | Before | Which sentence is correct? | The window broke by the storm. | The window was broken by the storm. ✔ | The passive is "be" plus V3. "Broke" is the past tense, not V3. |
| Passive voice | After | Which sentence is correct? | The results will announce next week. | The results will be announced next week. ✔ | After "will", keep "be" and add V3. |
| Gerunds and infinitives | Before | Which sentence is correct? | She suggested to go by MTR. | She suggested going by MTR. ✔ | "Suggest" is followed by -ing, never by "to". |
| Gerunds and infinitives | After | Which sentence is correct? | I'm looking forward to see you. | I'm looking forward to seeing you. ✔ | Here "to" is a preposition, and a preposition is followed by -ing. |
| Conditionals | Before | Which sentence is correct? | If it will rain tomorrow, we will stay home. | If it rains tomorrow, we will stay home. ✔ | Not after "if": the "if" part stays in the present. |
| Conditionals | After | Which sentence is correct? | If I would be you, I would apologise. | If I were you, I would apologise. ✔ | The "if" part of an unreal situation uses "were". "Would" belongs in the other part. |

## Text types (writing)

| Page | Slot | Question | A | B | Reveal [NEW] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Article | Before | Which opening suits a magazine article on stress? | Dear Editor, I am writing about stress. | Every exam season, the library fills up before the sun does. ✔ | An article hooks readers with a picture. "Dear Editor" starts a letter. |
| Article | After | Which ending gives the reader something to do? | In conclusion, stress is bad. | Next time the work piles up, close the book for ten minutes and walk. ✔ | End on one small thing the reader can try, not on a summary. |
| Letter to the editor | Before | Which opening fits a letter to a newspaper? | Hi everyone! | Dear Editor, ✔ | You write to the editor, so start "Dear Editor,". |
| Letter to the editor | After | Which sign-off goes with "Dear Editor"? | Yours sincerely, | Yours faithfully, ✔ | You do not know the name, so it is "faithfully". |
| Speech | Before | Which opening suits a speech? | This speech will talk about three points. | Good morning, everyone. Have you ever sat in class wishing you were somewhere else? ✔ | A speech is for the ear. Greet the audience, then ask something they can answer in their heads. |
| Speech | After | Which ending is stronger for a speech about volunteering? | That is all. Thank you. | So this Saturday, give two hours. Sign up at the hall. Thank you. ✔ | Finish with what you want the audience to do, then thank them. |
| Proposal | Before | Which sounds like a proposal? | I think we should have a reading corner because it is good. | I propose a reading corner in Room 204, using two spare bookcases. ✔ | A proposal wins by being practical: where, and with what. |
| Proposal | After | Which sentence answers "Why will it work?" | Students will love it. | It costs nothing, since the bookcases are already unused. ✔ | A committee wants a reason it can check, not a feeling. |
| Report | Before | Which sentence belongs under "Findings"? | In my opinion the canteen is too crowded. | Seventy-two of the 100 students surveyed queue for over ten minutes. ✔ | Findings are facts. Opinions come last and are labelled. |
| Report | After | Which sentence belongs under "Recommendations"? | Three-quarters of students queue too long. | The canteen should open a second counter at lunch. ✔ | A recommendation says what to do. A finding says what was found. |
| Story | Before | Which shows the feeling? | I was very nervous. | My hands were cold, and the words on the page would not stay still. ✔ | Show what the body does. Do not just name the feeling. |
| Story | After | Which ending is stronger? | Then I woke up. It was all a dream. | I walked off the stage with my hands still shaking, and smiling anyway. ✔ | A dream ending feels like a cheat. End on a real moment. |

## Things to check

- The numbers in the report items (72 of 100, ten minutes) are made up for the example. Say if you want them removed.
- "Yours faithfully" with "Dear Editor" follows the usual Paper 2 convention; confirm it matches what you teach.
- Each Text types page leads with its own plan, so the "Before" question must not give the plan away. Say if any item does.
- Sentences in the Tenses and Passive items are grade-appropriate for S.4; swap any you think are too easy.
