# Vocab flashcards and quiz: card content (proposal for CLY's review, not built)

The S.3 Vocab page becomes a hub: **Flashcards**, **Quiz**, and the existing cloze passage. Three decks: Unit 1 (23 words), Unit 2 (22 words) and Phrases (31). Words keep the meaning and word family already in `vocab-data.js`; this draft adds a collocation, a sample sentence and a "Good for" line to each. Sample sentences are CLY's own and are what the quiz blanks out, so each is written to make exactly one answer fit. Edit freely.

**Topics** are the Toolkit's seven (Education, Technology, Environment, Health, Youth, Culture, Work). **Text types** are the six on the Text types pages (Article, Letter, Speech, Proposal, Report, Story). A word with no natural topic shows text types only.

## How a card works

- **Front:** the word and its class. **Tap to flip.**
- **Back:** meaning, word family, collocation, sample sentence, Good for.
- **Two buttons:** "Got it" and "Again". "Again" cards come back later in the same round. Progress is saved on the device only, and the deck shows "12 of 23 learned".
- **Phrase cards:** front is the phrase; back says what it does ("shows a cause"), then the sample sentence and Good for.

## How the quiz works

- Ten questions a round, drawn from the chosen deck. Each question is the card's sample sentence with the word blanked out and four choices: the right word in the right form, two other words from the same deck, and one wrong form from the same word family (for example *employment* beside *employ*).
- A wrong answer gives a hint (the meaning) and another try. A right answer on the first try scores a point.
- A round ends with a score and up to three stars (10 = three, 8 to 9 = two, 6 to 7 = one). A finished round counts as a practice set, so the existing badges are earned. No timer, no lives, no leaderboard, no new badge art.

## Phrases (31): professional language for Paper 2 and Paper 3

Chosen because they are normal in reports, proposals, emails and speeches. Stock openers that markers have read too often ("Nowadays", "In today's society", "As we all know") and picture-clichés ("a double-edged sword", "the tip of the iceberg") are left out on purpose. The formal email and speech closings ("I look forward to hearing from you", "Thank you for your attention") are kept because they are always used in a professional context. Several come from phrases already on the Toolkit pages.

## Changes made while building

- **Sentences reworded** so the word is in its base form and no article gives the answer away: employ, utilize, engaging, light-hearted, unconventional, chuckle, improvise, lend a hand, turn to. The built versions are in `assets/js/data/vocab-cards.js`, which is now the source of truth.
- **Quiz choices are hand-written** for every card (three wrong choices, clearly wrong in the sentence), not drawn from the deck at random, because random choices sometimes also fit the gap.
- **Practice-set rule:** the first quiz round with 6 or more first-try answers in each deck counts as a practice set (three possible), so repeating a round does not inflate the badges.
