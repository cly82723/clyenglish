One sentence, five levels

Here is the same idea written five ways. The task: should schools ban mobile phones? Tap a level to see the sentence change, and read the note to see why it scores higher.

Topic sentence: phones in class

Tap a levelClick a level or use ← → keys

Level 2

I think phone is bad for student because they play it in class.

The idea is there, but the grammar gets in the way: phone and student need to be plural, and "play it" is unclear.

Level 3

In my opinion, mobile phones are bad for students because they always play games in class.

Accurate, but generic. "Bad" and "always" tell the marker nothing specific. Every Level 3 script sounds like this.

Level 4

Mobile phones distract students in class, as many find it hard to resist checking their messages during lessons.

A precise verb, "distract", replaces "bad". The reason is now a real situation, not a guess.

Level 5

Although phones can be useful learning tools, they often distract students, who find it hard to ignore a notification in the middle of a lesson.

It admits the other side first ("Although…"), then makes the point. That balance is what examiners call a mature argument.

Level 5**

A phone in a pocket is a lesson half-heard: even when students resist the urge to check it, every buzz pulls a little attention away from the teacher.

A short, memorable image carries the argument. The writer controls rhythm with a colon and makes a point other candidates miss: the phone distracts even when it is ignored.

Notice that no level uses fancy words for the sake of it. Each step is more precise, not more complicated.

What the marker reads for

Both parts are marked in three areas. Knowing them tells you where your next marks are hiding.

Content

Did you answer the question you were given? Are your ideas relevant, developed, and suited to the reader?

Quickest win: give every main point an example or a reason.

Language

Is your grammar accurate? Is your vocabulary precise? Do your sentences vary?

Quickest win: fix your three most common errors before anything else.

Organisation

Does the writing flow? Are paragraphs clear? Does the format match the text type?

Quickest win: one idea per paragraph, with a clear opening sentence.
