Paper 3 · Part B2 · Skill 5 of 5

Use the source in your own words.

Before you read: Which uses the 2022 source line better?

The first copies the line and ignores the reader. The second uses the same fact, in your own words, for the person you are writing to.

By the end you can keep the facts and change the words and the tone.

Three moves

  • Swap a word for a close one: “interfere with” becomes “disturb”.
  • Change the shape: a rule becomes a promise to the reader.
  • Join it to what the reader asked about.
  • Do not change a name, a number, a date or a technical term. They are the points.

Change the tone too

In 2022 the minutes told the writer to avoid blaming language when answering worried residents. In 2024 the handbook says an incident report should present the Review Board's results and, if appropriate, assign blame. Read each document for how the text should sound, not only what it should say.

Check every point

Use your list from step 7 of Skill 2: every point answered, every point in your own words, every fact the same as the source.

Try it: choose the best rewrite

2024 minutes: “Ms Goh suggested that participants should be referred to the company website for the itineraries.” You are writing the email to participants.

Not quite. Have another look.

The second keeps the fact (itineraries, website), speaks to the reader, and is complete. This is point 9.9.

2024 tapescript: lifetime membership gives “a 30% discount on all tours”. Which rewrite keeps the fact?

Not quite. Have another look.

Keep the number and what it applies to: tours, for life (point 9.4). The others change the fact.

2024: the Review Board concluded Captain Moby was not at fault and that he was extremely professional. Which sentence for the incident report keeps both facts?

Not quite. Have another look.

Both facts are there (points 8.6 and 8.8) in the report's own words, and it keeps the Board's key words.

2022 EPD guideline: “Light emitted must not exceed levels which interfere with human sleep.” Which sentence is the better reply to the residents?

Not quite. Have another look.

It keeps the fact, changes the words, and answers the residents' worry.
