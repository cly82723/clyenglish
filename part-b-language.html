Part B: all five steps

Step 4 · Language

Replace vague words with precise ones.

Before you read

Which sentence tells the reader more?

Bad how? In the first the reader has to guess. “Distract” answers it in one word.

By the end you can swap a vague word for the exact one.

Language marks reward words that are accurate and exact, not words that are long. Swap every vague word for the one that says exactly what you mean.

  1. Find the vague wordUsually good, bad, many, things, very or a lot.
  2. Ask the reader's next questionBad how? How many? Which things?
  3. Answer it in the sentenceAn exact verb, a number, or the things themselves, named.
  4. Check it is still correctA plain word used correctly beats a rare word used wrongly.

1 The example

One sentence, three levels

3 The vague word

Mobile phones are bad for students.

Bad how? The reader has to guess.

5 One exact verb

Mobile phones distract students in class.

"Distract" answers "bad how?" in one word.

5** The exact word, made into a scene

A phone buzzing under the desk can pull a student out of a lesson three or four times an hour.

The reader can see it, and can check the number against their own school day.

2 Try it

Swap the vague word

The vague words are marked. Tap the swap that makes each sentence exact.

  1. The canteen food is bad.

  2. Many students in my class have part-time jobs.

  3. Social media has a big effect on teenagers.

  4. The new MTR line is good for residents.

  5. Teenagers today worry about a lot of things.

  6. Recycling is very important.

3 Why this works

Why exact words score

Language marks reward accuracy and range, not rare words. "Distract" is an everyday word, but it does a job that "bad" cannot.

An exact word answers the reader's next question before they ask it. A vague word leaves them to fill the gap, and markers do not fill gaps for you.

A stronger word is not a more exact word. "Terrible" and "huge" only shout louder; they still don't say how, or how many.

CLY: If you could swap your word for "good" or "bad" and the sentence still made sense, it isn't exact yet.
