Part B: all five steps

Step 3 · Organisation

Build every body paragraph in four moves: Claim, Reason, Evidence, Impact.

Before you read

Which last sentence says why the paragraph matters?

The first repeats the opening line, so the marker learns nothing new. The second is the Impact: what happens because of the point.

By the end you can build a body paragraph in four moves: Claim, Reason, Evidence, Impact.

  • CClaim Your point, in one sentence.
  • RReason Why it is true.
  • EEvidence Proof: a fact, a figure or a scene from Hong Kong.
  • IImpact Why it matters to the reader.

1 The example

Should secondary schools start at 9 a.m. instead of 8 a.m.?

One paragraph, three levels

Tap a levelClick a level or use ← → keys

Level 3

CFirstly, schools should start later. EFor example, many students sleep very late because they have a lot of homework. CSo schools should start later.

No reason and no impact. The last sentence repeats the first.

Level 5

CFirst, a later start would improve students' health. RTeenagers' body clocks naturally run later, so asking them to be alert at 8 a.m. works against their biology. EMany S5 students in my school sleep after midnight because of homework and tutorial classes, yet leave home before 7. IAs a result, they arrive tired and learn less in their first lessons.

All four moves, in order. The example is real but general.

Level 5**

CThe strongest case for a later start is not comfort but learning. RSleep research shows that a teenager's body clock runs up to two hours later than an adult's, so an 8 a.m. lesson asks students to think at the hour their brains are least ready. EWalk into any S5 classroom at 8:15 and the evidence is in front of you: heads on desks, a quiz half-finished, a teacher repeating instructions for the third time. IIf the first two lessons of every day are lost to fatigue, we are paying full price for an education students only half receive.

All four moves, each one sharper. The example is a scene you can see.

2 Try it

Practise: label each sentence

Each paragraph below is jumbled. Tap C, R, E or I for every sentence, then check.

Task: should phones be allowed in lessons?

  1. When my English teacher let us check collocations on our phones during a writing lesson, the whole class corrected mistakes we had been making all term.

  2. Schools should allow phones in lessons, but only as learning tools.

  3. Banning phones outright would throw away a tool that, used well, makes students more independent learners.

  4. Used with clear rules, a phone gives every student instant access to dictionaries, news and practice tools that a textbook cannot match.

Task: should the plastic levy cover takeaway containers?

  1. A small charge changes habits because people notice the cost every single time they pay it.

  2. Applying the same idea to containers could cut one of the largest sources of waste in our landfills.

  3. The government should extend the plastic levy to all takeaway containers.

  4. After Hong Kong's plastic bag levy began, carrying a reusable bag quickly became normal at the supermarket checkout.

Task: should schools keep school uniforms?

  1. At my school, students who used to be teased for cheap trainers now look exactly like everyone else.

  2. Keeping uniforms protects students from a quiet form of bullying that money would otherwise decide.

  3. Schools should keep school uniforms.

  4. A uniform removes the daily pressure to compete over expensive clothes and brands.

Task: should the government build more cycling tracks?

  1. Safe tracks make cycling a realistic way to make short journeys instead of taking a car.

  2. The government should build more cycling tracks in the new towns.

  3. More tracks would cut traffic and give residents a cheap way to stay healthy.

  4. In Sha Tin, the riverside track is packed every weekend with families and commuters on bikes.

Task: should schools set homework at weekends?

  1. A homework-free weekend would send students back on Monday ready to learn rather than simply tired.

  2. Students need unbroken time to rest; otherwise they start each week already exhausted.

  3. Many S5 students spend their Sundays finishing worksheets instead of seeing their families.

  4. Schools should not set homework at weekends.

Task: should Hong Kong host more international sports events?

  1. Hong Kong should host more international sports events.

  2. During the Hong Kong Sevens, the restaurants near the stadium are full all weekend.

  3. Major events attract visitors who spend money in hotels, restaurants and shops.

  4. Hosting more events would boost the economy and give young athletes role models to look up to.

Task: should secondary schools teach cooking?

  1. Cooking is a basic life skill that helps young people eat more healthily.

  2. Teaching cooking now would give students the independence they will need the day they leave home.

  3. Secondary schools should teach students how to cook.

  4. When my cousin moved into university halls, she lived on instant noodles because she had never learnt to cook.

Task: should fast-food restaurants show calorie counts?

  1. When a burger shop near my school started showing calories on its menu, many of my classmates switched to smaller meals.

  2. Fast-food restaurants should display calorie counts on their menus.

  3. Clear labels would help the city tackle obesity without banning any food.

  4. People make better choices when they can see what they are about to eat.

Task: should every student do community service?

  1. Every secondary student should do community service.

  2. Community service would turn students into citizens who notice the needs around them.

  3. After volunteering at a food bank in Kwun Tong, my class started a monthly food drive of its own.

  4. Helping others teaches responsibility in a way that classroom lessons cannot.

Task: should some public libraries open 24 hours a day?

  1. Students who live in small flats often end up studying in fast-food restaurants late at night.

  2. Many students have no quiet space to study at home in the evening.

  3. Some public libraries should open 24 hours a day.

  4. Round-the-clock libraries would give these students a safe, free place to work.

3 Why this works

Why the 5** paragraph scores higher

The Level 3 paragraph goes in a circle. It states the claim, gives a vague example, and states the claim again. A marker learns nothing new after the first sentence.

The Level 5 paragraph is complete and logical. Its impact stops at "learn less", which is true of any tired student anywhere.

The Level 5** paragraph answers the obvious objection first: that a later start is only about comfort. Its impact turns tiredness into a cost everyone cares about.

Reports and stories do not use CREI. They have their own patterns: see Text types.
