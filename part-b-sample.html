Part B: the long task

The essentials

Five steps. Each is one thing to do, with one example of doing it.

  1. 0

    Step 0 · Choose and plan

    Choose the question you can find three points for. Then plan for ten minutes.

    "I like sport, so I'll do the sports question."
    "I'll take the proposal: I have three points, and I know its format by heart."

  2. 1

    Step 1 · Content

    Use three different ideas, and give each one a Hong Kong example.

    For example, many people want to do exercise.
    In districts like Sham Shui Po, where public courts are often full, a school court open until nine…

  3. 2

    Step 2 · Organisation

    Build every body paragraph in four moves: Claim, Reason, Evidence, Impact.

    CA later start would improve students' health. RTeenagers' body clocks run later. EMany S5 students sleep after midnight, yet leave home before 7. IThey arrive tired and learn less.

    See the CREI lesson

  4. 3

    Step 3 · Language

    Replace vague words with precise ones.

    Mobile phones are bad for students.
    Mobile phones distract students in class.

  5. 4

    Step 4 · Put it together

    Follow your text type's format, and answer every part of the question.

    A proposal needs To, From, Date and Subject, then headings.

    See the Proposal page

CREI paragraphs

Every body paragraph makes four moves, in this order.

  • CClaim Your point, in one sentence.
  • RReason Why it is true.
  • EEvidence Proof: a fact, a figure or a scene from Hong Kong.
  • IImpact Why it matters to the reader.

The example

Should secondary schools start at 9 a.m. instead of 8 a.m.?

One paragraph, three levels

Tap a levelClick a level or use ← → keys

Level 3

CFirstly, schools should start later. EFor example, many students sleep very late because they have a lot of homework. CSo schools should start later.

No reason and no impact. The last sentence repeats the first.

Level 5

CFirst, a later start would improve students' health. RTeenagers' body clocks naturally run later, so asking them to be alert at 8 a.m. works against their biology. EMany S5 students in my school sleep after midnight because of homework and tutorial classes, yet leave home before 7. IAs a result, they arrive tired and learn less in their first lessons.

All four moves, in order. The example is real but general.

Level 5**

CThe strongest case for a later start is not comfort but learning. RSleep research shows that a teenager's body clock runs up to two hours later than an adult's, so an 8 a.m. lesson asks students to think at the hour their brains are least ready. EWalk into any S5 classroom at 8:15 and the evidence is in front of you: heads on desks, a quiz half-finished, a teacher repeating instructions for the third time. IIf the first two lessons of every day are lost to fatigue, we are paying full price for an education students only half receive.

All four moves, each one sharper. The example is a scene you can see.

Try it

Take one paragraph you have written and tick each line that is true of it.

Then label the sentences in five sets of jumbled paragraphs. In the finished guide that drill sits here.

Why this works

The Level 3 paragraph goes in a circle. It states the claim, gives a vague example, and states the claim again. A marker learns nothing new after the first sentence.

The Level 5 paragraph is complete and logical. Its impact stops at "learn less", which is true of any tired student anywhere.

The Level 5** paragraph answers the obvious objection first: that a later start is only about comfort. Its impact turns tiredness into a cost everyone cares about.

Reports and stories do not use CREI. They have their own patterns, on the Text types page.

Proposal

A proposal asks someone with power to approve a plan. It wins by being practical.

Reader
A decision-maker
Tone
Formal and businesslike
Format
To · From · Date · Subject, then headings

Paragraph by paragraph

  1. Header

    Says who it is to, who it is from, the date and the subject.

    To: The Principal · From: Chris Wong, Student Union · Date · Subject: Proposal for a reading corner

  2. Introduction

    States the purpose in one or two sentences.

    The purpose of this proposal is to suggest a reading corner in the covered playground.

  3. Background

    Shows the problem as it is now, with evidence: a survey or something you have seen.

  4. The plan

    Says what, where, when, who, and what it costs.

    The estimated cost is $3,000, which could be covered by the Student Union budget.

  5. Benefits

    Says who gains and how. Build each benefit with CREI.

    This would allow students without quiet space at home to read before lessons.

  6. Conclusion

    Makes a polite, specific request for approval.

    We would be grateful if you could consider this proposal at the next committee meeting.

The example

"The plan" section: a reading corner

Tap a levelClick a level or use ← → keys

Level 3

We want to make a reading corner. It will have many books and sofas. Students will like it.

A wish, not a plan. Where? Who pays? Who looks after it?

Level 5

We propose converting the unused corner of the covered playground into a reading area with two bookshelves and floor cushions. Books will be donated by students, and Library Prefects will manage the area at lunchtime.

Place, materials, source and staffing are all answered.

Level 5**

The corner beside the covered playground is used only for storing PE mats, which can move to the gym. With two shelves and floor cushions (about $3,000 from the Student Union budget) and books donated through a class competition, it could open within a month and be staffed at lunchtime by Library Prefects, at no cost to teachers.

It solves the objections before the principal raises them: space, cost, timing and staff time.

Final reminders

  • Use headings. A busy principal should be able to skim them.
  • Use "would" and "could" to sound reasonable: "This would allow…"
  • Answer space, cost, timing and staff before the reader asks.
  • In the exam, invent a reasonable cost figure.
  • Emotional pleading: "Please, please approve this, students really need it!"
More sentences to borrow

  • We propose that … (should) + base verb

    We propose that the corner (should) be opened at lunchtime from next month.

    Formal suggestion: the verb stays in its base form.

  • Should the plan be approved, …

    Should the plan be approved, the corner could open within a month.

    An inverted conditional: formal and confident.

A past-paper task, reviewed: 2016 Q7

The Hong Kong Youth Association is organising a Youth Festival to showcase popular youth trends in Hong Kong. Write a proposal suggesting one or two trends popular with young people, how they could be presented at the Festival, and why.

  • Pick trends you can show, not just describe: street art, sustainable fashion, e-sports, cafés and latte art, hiking.
  • For each: what, how, why. What the trend is, how visitors experience it at the Festival, and why it deserves the space.
  • Describing the trend for 300 words and leaving "how to present it" to the last line.

What would be deleted

Everything else is kept: shown, folded or moved. These are the only passages I would remove, from the parts rewritten so far.

  1. "Why, how, so what" in The content methodThe heading "Develop each one: why, how, so what", its ladder notes and the closing line. CREI teaches the same three moves as Reason, Evidence and Impact.
  2. "The example, three levels" in The content methodA second ladder that repeats, in shorter form, the three sentences of the ladder just above it.
  3. "The last sentence, three levels" in CREIIt repeats the Impact sentences already shown in the main example.
  4. The level-card headings in CREI"Claim and a vague example, then the claim again" and the two like it. The coloured C, R, E and I marks already show this.
  5. "Reports and stories use their own patterns; see Text-type features."Replaced by one line pointing to the Text types page.
