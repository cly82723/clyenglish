Part B: the long task

Five steps, from choosing a question to the last five minutes. Each is one short page about one skill.

Not sure where to start? Try three questions

Tap the stronger line each time. Nothing is marked, and every step stays open to you.

Quick check

Content: which gives the reader more to picture?

The second names a place the reader can picture. The first could be about anywhere.

Quick check

Organisation: which paragraph has a claim, a reason, an example and why it matters?

The second has all four moves, in order. The first has no reason and no impact, and its last sentence repeats its first.

Quick check

Language: which sentence says exactly why?

The second says why. “Very important” leaves the reader to guess.

1 Step 1 · Choose and planChoose the question you can find three points for. Then plan for ten minutes. Done Weaker: "I like sport, so I'll do the sports question." Stronger: "I'll take the proposal: I have three points, and I know its format by heart." 2 Step 2 · ContentUse three different ideas, and give each one a Hong Kong example. Done Weaker: For example, many people want to do exercise. Stronger: In districts like Sham Shui Po, where public courts are often full, a school court open until nine… 3 Step 3 · OrganisationBuild every body paragraph in four moves: Claim, Reason, Evidence, Impact. Done CA later start would improve students' health. RTeenagers' body clocks run later. EMany S5 students sleep after midnight, yet leave home before 7. IThey arrive tired and learn less. 4 Step 4 · LanguageReplace vague words with precise ones. Done Weaker: Mobile phones are bad for students. Stronger: Mobile phones distract students in class. 5 Step 5 · Put it togetherFollow your text type's format, and answer every part of the question. Done A proposal needs To, From, Date and Subject, then headings.
