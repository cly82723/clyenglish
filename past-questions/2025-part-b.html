Q2Story

Your friend experienced an event that significantly changed him. Write a short story about this.

CLY's ideas and sample answers

Your friend experienced an event that significantly changed him. Write a short story about this.

All three candidates set their story at a theme park and meet an old schoolmate who has changed, so you can compare them directly. The stories are long, so key passages are shown, with the rest summarised in square brackets.

The story at three levels, and a rewrite

Tap a levelClick a level or use ← → keys

Real exam script · HKEAA exemplar

Recently, I am having work at a theme park, Ocean Park. Today, I back to Ocean Park to work as normal. When I was having my duty, I saw an old classmate, Chris Wong, who behaved badly when I was at school in Ocean Park too. I was surprised and wondering why he is a little bit different, he seems like softer and friendly than before, is it my illusion? In my memory, Chris Wong always mad and love fighting with others. Then I told him my thought, and he told me about an important event that happened last year and this made him a better person.

[Chris left school, was fired from a restaurant for being rude, attacked his manager and was sent to jail. A social worker found that he had copied his violent father, and helped him.]

…The social worker helped him to pass through the mental illness slowly. She healed his heart. After a month, Chris recovered and he became a better person.

This is the story that why Chris become a better person. And Chris told me, now he will not fight with others anymore and he also can control his emotion. He said, "I am very thankful. Without the social worker, I can't be the better me."

HKEAA 2025 exemplars, Level 3 exemplar 1. Errors are underlined.

Content
A complete story of change with a clear cause (the social worker). But almost everything is summarised, not shown, and the change is simply stated: "he became a better person" appears three times.
Organisation
Clear and chronological. The theme-park meeting opens the story but is never returned to, so the ending is a summary ("This is the story that…") instead of a final scene.
Language
Tense errors throughout ("I am having work", "I back to"), simple and repeated vocabulary, and almost no dialogue until the last line.

To reach Level 4: turn two of the summarised events into short scenes with dialogue, and fix the tenses: a story is told in the past.

Real exam script · HKEAA exemplar

CONSEQUENCES & PENITENCE

Halfway through the day, I found myself bored wandering about the theme park. I muttered to myself "why did I go here again?" as I remember, reaching through my pockets I see a crumpled up ticket from the letter coming from my mom. She was worried that I have stayed in my room the past week without any hint of sunlight…

By sheer coincidence, at the corner of my eye, I see a familiar face that I wish I could forget. It was this high-school bully that everyone hated… I put on a façade of politeness as I greet him: "Hey! How have you been!" "Wassup my man! Let me get you a drink at that restaurant over there."

…Taking a second look at him, he seems more polite and soft, although his tough exterior is still apparent. "Stuff happens, you know?" his tone lowered as a sombre expression washes over his face.

[He tells how an accident left his mother in a coma, how he worked to support her with no one to help, and how he has been asking everyone he hurt to forgive him.]

I guess karma does exist, the golden rule of "you reap what you sow" is actually true and kindness is met with kindness, and of course vice versa.

~ THE END ~

HKEAA 2025 exemplars, Level 4 exemplar 1.

Content
A real character with a backstory, and a change that is partly shown ("more polite and soft, although his tough exterior is still apparent"). The ending falls back on a moral cliché ("you reap what you sow") instead of a moment.
Organisation
A strong set-up (the crumpled ticket, the unwanted meeting) and a flashback told through dialogue. The second half rushes, and the opening's ticket returns only briefly.
Language
Lively, idiomatic phrases ("put on a façade of politeness", "a sombre expression washes over his face"), but the tense jumps between present and past almost every sentence.

What moved it up from Level 3: scenes and dialogue instead of summary, and vivid phrases. To reach Level 5: keep one tense, and end on a scene instead of a moral.

Real exam script · HKEAA exemplar

Life On A Ride

"Brother, brother," my younger sister, Mary, pulled on my sleeve, "I want to ride that."

I glanced over to the ride she was pointing towards. A neon sign over the entrance says 'Rusty Rumble' in bold letters. I looked down and saw a sign that says 'Expected queue time: 60 minutes'. Suddenly, the neon sign did not look appealing at all…

"Chris?" as I arrived to the queue, I heard a familiar voice call out to me. I looked up and gasped in shock. He certainly looked… different, to say the least. Back in the days when we were classmates, he dyed his fiery red hair and had piercings all over his body. His breath used to reek of the stench of cigarettes. Now, he's back to his natural hair colour, and there's not a single piercing in sight.

Peter chuckled, "Yeah, life's been a heck of a ride."

[In the queue, Peter tells how anxiety and depression, and parents who would not listen, led to a hospital stay; how the friends he made there changed how he saw things; and how his family was waiting for him when he came out. He is now repeating Form 6.]

He returned the hug and didn't say anything, but I could feel him smiling. His warmth no longer came with the stench of cigarettes, but radiated courage and determination to change and become a better person.

"Next!" the ride operator shouted towards the queue. Mary, Peter, and I moved onto the ride. As the seatbelts fastened, Mary squeezed my hand…

"Don't be scared," I comforted her, "For now, it's only going up!"

"Yeah, only going up," Peter said. "Even if it falls, you'll be back here safe and sound in no time."

HKEAA 2025 exemplars, Level 5 exemplar 1. The middle section deals with mental illness and is summarised.

Content
The change is shown through details you can see and smell (red hair, piercings, cigarettes → natural hair, no piercings), and the serious theme is handled with care. The ride becomes a metaphor for Peter's life, signalled in the title and in "life's been a heck of a ride".
Organisation
A frame story: it opens in the queue, Peter's story happens while they wait, and it closes on the ride itself. The last lines ("only going up… even if it falls") echo his whole story.
Language
Natural dialogue that moves the story, vivid sensory detail, and controlled punctuation. Small slips remain ("I arrived to the queue", tense shifts in the signs).

What moved it up from Level 4: a frame and a metaphor that hold the story together, change shown through detail, and an ending that is a scene, not a moral.

The Level 5 story has a 5** structure. What would lift it is tighter sentences and a final image that lands. Here are its reveal and ending, rewritten by CLY.

CLY's rewrite · not an exam script

"Chris?" The voice came from the queue behind us, and it took me a moment to place it. The last time I had seen Peter, his hair was fire-engine red, there were rings in both eyebrows, and he smelt of the cigarettes he smoked behind the school hall. The boy in front of me had black hair, a plain grey hoodie and nothing in his hands but a ride ticket. Only his grin was the same.

[…Peter's story, told in the queue…]

"Next!" the operator called. As the bar clicked down across our laps, Mary grabbed my hand. "It's only going up for now," I told her. Beside us, Peter laughed and loosened his grip on the rail. "And when it drops," he said, "you still end up back where you started, only a little braver."

What changed
The reveal is one tight paragraph of contrasts, ending on the one thing that has not changed. The final image (Peter letting go of the rail) shows his change in an action, and his last line finishes the ride metaphor.

Q4EssayWork

Discuss the challenges of group work in the workplace and provide suggestions for improvement. Write an essay about this.

CLY's ideas and sample answers

Discuss the challenges of group work in the workplace and provide suggestions for improvement. Write an essay about this.

The ideas

  • Challenges: clashing schedules and working styles; slow decisions and endless meetings; nobody feeling responsible; conflict between members.
  • Suggestions: one named owner per task; clear deadlines; shared online tools; short regular check-ins.
  • Pair them: every suggestion should solve a challenge you have already described. A list of tips that answers nothing loses Content marks.

From the official exemplars: the same idea at two levels

Both scripts open with the same challenge: team members work at different times.

3 "One of the major challenges of group work in the workplace is that employees have different working schedule."

HKEAA 2025 exemplars, Level 3 exemplar 3 (the candidate's grammar is kept)

5 "For example, a member who prefers working at night would find it difficult to agree on a meeting time with another member who likes working during the day."

5 "When tasked as a group, individuals tend to feel less responsible for the task, as they can easily push the responsibility onto another member."

HKEAA 2025 exemplars, Level 5 exemplar 3

What changed: the Level 3 writer names the challenge. The Level 5 writer shows it with two specific people, then finds a deeper challenge most candidates miss: when a task belongs to everyone, nobody feels responsible for it.

From a published 5** sample

"When one individual falls behind, the entire group bears the consequences."

Sample on PL Learning

What works: one short sentence carries a whole cause and effect, which makes a strong topic sentence. The same sample pairs each challenge with a practical fix, such as named roles, fixed deadlines and shared planning tools.

One step further: the same idea at 5**

5** The Level 5 idea, named, shown and solved

The quietest problem in any team is the one no one owns. When a task belongs to five people, each can assume that someone else will chase the supplier or check the figures, and on the morning of the deadline the team discovers that everyone assumed. Psychologists call this diffusion of responsibility; managers simply call it a missed deadline. Naming one owner for every task, however small, costs nothing and removes the place where work goes to hide.

A memorable claim, a scene from a real office, the proper name for the effect, and a suggestion that answers exactly this challenge.
