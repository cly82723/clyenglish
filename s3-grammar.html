2 Defining and non-defining noun phrasesextra information about a noun

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

It is extra information, so it needs a comma on both sides.

One check: Cover the phrase with your finger. If you still know exactly who or what the sentence means, use commas. If not, use no commas.

See the rules

Quiz

Cover the phrase with your finger each time: do you still know who or what is meant?

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

Which president? The name is needed here, so it can't be left out.

3 Bare infinitiveslet me go, made me tidy (no “to”)

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

"Let" + someone + V1, with no "to".

One check: Find can, must, should, had better, would rather, make or let. Then check the next verb (or the verb after the person): it must be plain V1.

See the rules

Quiz

Find the word that switches off "to". The tense of the sentence doesn't change the verb after it.

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

Both verbs are V1: would rather + V1 + than + V1.

Unit 2

4 So, so that and so as tosaying why you do something

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

Don't drop "as": the phrase is "so as to".

One check: Ask: did it happen, or was it someone's plan? Happened: so. Plan: look after the gap. A subject needs "so that"; a verb alone needs "so as to".

See the rules

Quiz

Ask whether it really happened or was only the aim, then look at what follows the blank.

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

"Not" goes before "to", and "to" appears only once.

5 Must, have to and need torules and choices

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

After "must", use V1 with no "to".

One check: When you see "must not", ask: is this really forbidden? If the person is simply free to choose, you need "don't have to" or "don't need to".

See the rules

Quiz

In negative sentences, ask: is it forbidden, or is it a free choice?

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

It is the person's choice, not a ban, so use "don't have to".

Unit 3

7 Past perfectthe thing that happened first

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

The bus left first, so it takes had + V3.

One check: Look for two past events and ask which came first. Only that one gets "had". If there is only one past event, "had" is a mistake.

See the rules

Quiz

Some options are grammatical but change the meaning. Wrong answers get a hint and another try.

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

There is no earlier past event, so a single finished action takes the past simple.

8 Present participle phrasesWalking home, I saw…

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

Make a present participle phrase negative with "not", never "don't".

One check: After a present participle phrase, look at the first noun after the comma and ask: can this do the V-ing action? If not, the sentence is broken.

See the rules

Quiz

Watch for sentences where the wrong person (or thing) is doing the action.

Rewrite practice

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

The mountains weren't looking out of the window. The person doing the looking must come after the comma.

9 Past participle phrasesExcited about the trip, we…

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

The children felt excited. The person with the feeling takes -ed.

One check: When a sentence starts with V-ing or V3, check the subject after the comma. Is it doing the action (V-ing) or receiving it (V3)? Is it the right subject at all?

See the rules

Quiz

Ask each time: is the subject doing the action, or is it done to the subject?

Rewrite practice

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

Drop "so": the phrase already gives the reason. "Being" isn't needed either.

Unit 4

11 Reported statementstelling what someone said

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

"Say" can't take a person straight after it. Use "told me" or "said to me".

One check: After "said" or "told", check three things: the tense, the pronoun and the time word. One of them is usually the trap.

See the rules

Quiz

Read the situation first: when was it said, and is it still true? That decides what changes.

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

No quotation marks in reported speech, and "I" becomes "she".

12 Reported questionstelling what someone asked

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

Drop "did" and put the subject before the verb.

One check: After "asked", "wondered" or "Do you know", read what follows. If the verb comes before the subject, swap them.

See the rules

Quiz

Check the word order and the full stop or question mark at the end. Both can be the trap.

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

Inside a bigger question, use statement order: subject, then verb.

14 Linking wordsalthough, because, in case

Before you read

Which sentence is correct?

Use one contrast word. "Although" already links the two parts.

One check: For every linking word, check two things: does its job match the ideas (add, contrast, cause …)? And does the right grammar follow it (a noun or a clause, a new sentence or the same one)?

See the rules

Quiz

Choose the job first, then check what follows the blank: a noun, a verb, or a whole clause.

Check yourself

Which sentence is correct?

No "will" after "in case", just like after "if".

15 Tenses

The weekly challenge can use any of these. Look for the clue words next to the blank, then choose the form. For a negative, add not or n't.

The tenses, their forms and clue words
TenseFormClue wordsExample
Present simpleV1, or V1 + severy day, usually, oftenShe walks to school every day.
Present continuousam / is / are + V-ingnow, right now, at the momentWe are having lunch now.
Past simpleV2 (the past form)yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020The bus left ten minutes ago.
Past continuouswas / were + V-ingwhile, when, at eight last nightI was reading when she called.
Present perfecthave / has + V3since, for, already, yetHe has lived here since 2010.
Past perfecthad + V3by the time, before, afterBy the time we arrived, the film had started.
Futurewill + V1, or am / is / are going to + V1tomorrow, next week, soonWe will visit Grandma next week.
