S.3 Grammar · Units 3 & 4

Past perfect

Six topics, each with the rules in plain words and a quiz. The quizzes test what sounds natural, not just what fits a formula, so read every option to the end. Finish with the Challenge. Looking for vocab? S.3 vocab revision.

Quiz

Some options are grammatical but change the meaning. Wrong answers get a hint and another try.

Three versions

3When I arrived, the film has started.

5When I arrived, the film had already started.

5**By the time I had found my seat, the opening scene I'd waited months to see was already over.

CLY: Put "had" on the earlier event only. One "had" in the right place beats three in the wrong ones.

-ing phrases

Quiz

Watch for sentences where the wrong person (or thing) is doing the action.

Three versions

3Walking to school, the rain started.

5Walking to school, I got caught in the rain.

5**Sprinting the last hundred metres, I reached the school gate soaked but, for once, on time.

CLY: Check that the person after the comma is the one doing the -ing.

-ed phrases

Quiz

Ask each time: is the subject doing the action, or is it done to the subject?

Three versions

3Boring by the lesson, I fell asleep.

5Bored by the lesson, I fell asleep.

5**Exhausted by a week of mock exams, I fell asleep before the first slide had even loaded.

CLY: Feelings you have take -ed: bored, excited, worried. The thing that causes them takes -ing.

Reported statements

Quiz

Read the situation first: when was it said, and is it still true? That decides what changes.

Three versions

3He said me he will come.

5He told me he would come.

5**He promised he would come, but admitted he might be a little late.

CLY: Swap "said" for a verb that shows how it was said: promised, admitted, complained.

Reported questions

Quiz

Check the word order and the full stop or question mark at the end. Both can be the trap.

Three versions

3She asked me where did I go.

5She asked me where I had gone.

5**She asked me, a little too casually, where I had been on the night the trophy disappeared.

CLY: After "asked", write it like a statement: subject, then verb.

Linking words

Quiz

Choose the job first, then check what follows the blank: a noun, a verb, or a whole clause.

Three versions

3Although it rained, but we still went out.

5Although it rained, we still went out.

5**Despite the downpour, we went out anyway. After all, a little rain had never stopped us before.

CLY: One linker per link. Pick its job first, then check the grammar that follows it.

Challenge

All six topics, mixed, in longer texts. There are no topic labels to help you, so you have to decide which rule each gap needs.

Cloze

Put the word in brackets into the right form. You may need the past perfect, an -ing or -ed form, or a reported verb, and sometimes words like "not" or "never" are given too.

Proofreading

Most lines have one mistake; a few are correct. Some lines that look wrong are fine, so think before you change anything.
