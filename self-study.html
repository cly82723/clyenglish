Beyond the exam

Past papers teach you the exam. Reading things you enjoy teaches you English. The students who reach 5** usually do both.

Strong writers have read a lot of good sentences. They know how a sentence should sound because they've heard thousands of them. You can't get that from a vocabulary list. You get it from reading, listening and noticing, a little every day.

And English opens doors that have nothing to do with marks: games, music, films, friendships with people across the world, and university courses taught in English.

  • Choose what you read by interest, not by difficulty. Football, K-pop, science, cooking: it all counts.
  • Read a little every day rather than a lot once a week.
  • Looking up every unknown word. Guess from context, and look up only the words you see twice.

What to read and listen to

Start with something just a little above your level. If you understand most of a page, it's the right level.

4–5Feature articles in English newspapers; opinion columnsShows how writers build an argument
5**Long-form magazine writing; novels in a genre you enjoyRhythm, voice and structure across a whole piece

Read like a writer: when a sentence makes you stop, ask how the writer did it. Then copy the move, not the words.

Keep a phrase notebook

Your own notebook beats any list online, because every phrase in it is one you chose.

  1. Write the whole phrase, not one wordNot "awareness" but "raise awareness of the issue".
  2. Copy the sentence you found it inContext shows you how to use it.
  3. Sort it by jobOpenings · linking · describing people · giving opinions · endings.
  4. Use it within a weekA phrase you've used once is a phrase you own.
  • Use a real exercise book or the notes app on your phone. Whatever you'll actually open.
  • Copying long word lists. Five phrases you use beat fifty you don't.

The weekly rewrite challenge

Once a week, take one paragraph you wrote and rewrite it one level higher.

  1. Pick a paragraphFrom homework, a past paper, or your diary.
  2. Find its levelCompare it with a level ladder for the same text type.
  3. Make one changeUse the highlighted move from the next level up. Only one.
  4. Read both aloudCan you hear the difference? Save the new version.

This week's paragraph. Rewrite it at the next level up:

"Nowadays, many people use social media. It is very popular among teenagers. There are many good points and bad points. In my opinion, it is good if we use it carefully."
