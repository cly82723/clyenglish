Words by topic

Seven topics that Part B questions keep returning to. Learn each word with the words it goes with, not on its own.

Before you read

Which phrase sounds more like a strong script?

The second uses equip with its usual partners: someone, with, skills. Every word on this page is shown with its partners.

1 Education & school life

  • alleviate + pressure / a burden / anxiety

    Cancelling one round of tests would alleviate the pressure on S6 students in the months before the DSE.

    3reduce stress → 5**alleviate the pressure of public exams

  • foster + independence / creativity / a sense of belonging

    Project work fosters independence because students must plan their own time.

  • cater to + the needs of / learners of different abilities From a real Level 5 script, 2024 Q4

    A single textbook cannot cater to the needs of every learner in a class of thirty.

    3suit all students → 5**cater to learners of different abilities

  • exam-oriented + culture / system / teaching

    In an exam-oriented culture, anything that is not tested is quickly dropped.

  • holistic + development / education

    Schools speak of holistic development, yet timetables are filled with drilling.

  • drill + students in / past papers

    Drilling past papers improves exam technique, but not understanding.

  • rote learning rely on / move away from + rote learning

    Rote learning helps students pass tests but rarely helps them apply what they know.

  • equip + someone with skills / confidence

    Debating equips students with the confidence to defend an opinion in public.

    3give students skills → 5**equip students with transferable skills

  • burn out risk / be close to + burning out

    Students who attend tutorials every evening risk burning out before the exam itself.

  • live up to + expectations / a reputation

    Many teenagers feel they can never live up to their parents' expectations.

  • a level playing field create / provide + a level playing field

    Free tutoring for low-income families would create a more level playing field.

  • extracurricular activities take part in / drop + extracurricular activities

    Extracurricular activities give quieter students a place to shine.

  • peer pressure give in to / resist + peer pressure

    Some students join tutorial classes not out of need but because they give in to peer pressure.

  • academic performance improve / undermine + academic performance

    Lack of sleep can quietly undermine academic performance.

See them at work

Hong Kong's exam-oriented culture does more than drill students in past papers; it decides what counts as learning. When every lesson is measured by marks, schools struggle to cater to the needs of students who learn by doing, and extracurricular activities are the first thing parents cut. A system built to foster independence would alleviate the pressure of public exams instead of adding to it, and give every student a fairer chance to live up to their own potential.

2 Technology & social media

  • addictive + design / feature / content

    Endless scrolling is an addictive feature designed to keep users on the app.

  • misinformation spread / combat + misinformation

    Misinformation spreads fastest when it confirms what readers already fear.

    3fake news is bad → 5**misinformation spreads faster than corrections

  • screen time cut down on / limit + screen time

    Cutting down on screen time before bed improves sleep.

  • go viral a post / a video + goes viral

    A video can go viral overnight, and so can a mistake.

  • invade + someone's privacy

    Monitoring apps may protect children, but they also invade their privacy.

  • digital footprint leave / manage + a digital footprint

    Every post adds to a digital footprint that future employers may see.

  • fall victim to + cyberbullying / scams

    Teenagers who fall victim to cyberbullying often suffer in silence, because the abuse follows them home.

  • a double-edged sword be + a double-edged sword

    Social media is a double-edged sword: it connects us and isolates us at the same time.

    3has good and bad points → 5**is a double-edged sword

  • verify + information / a source

    Students should learn to verify a source before sharing a post.

  • the digital divide widen / bridge + the digital divide

    Online lessons widened the digital divide between families with and without laptops.

See them at work

Social media is a double-edged sword. The same features that help a small bakery reach its followers also trap teenagers in endless scrolling, where misinformation spreads faster than anyone can verify a source. The problem is not technology itself but design: addictive features leave young users with longer screen time and a digital footprint they never meant to create.

3 Environment

  • curb + emissions / waste / consumption

    A charge on takeaway containers would help curb single-use waste.

    3stop pollution → 5**curb carbon emissions

  • single-use + plastics / cutlery / containers

    Many restaurants now offer metal cutlery instead of single-use plastics.

  • divert + waste from landfills

    Recycling food waste would divert it from our overflowing landfills.

  • carbon footprint reduce / shrink + your carbon footprint

    Taking the MTR instead of a taxi is a small way to reduce your carbon footprint.

  • sustainable + lifestyle / development / choice

    A sustainable lifestyle is not about perfection but about better habits.

  • renewable energy generate / invest in + renewable energy

    Solar panels on school roofs could generate renewable energy and teach physics at the same time.

  • conserve + energy / water / habitats

    Setting air-conditioners at 25.5°C helps conserve energy without making anyone uncomfortable.

  • raise awareness + of an issue

    Beach clean-ups raise awareness of how much plastic reaches the sea.

  • eco-friendly + alternative / product / packaging

    Eco-friendly alternatives are often more expensive, which slows their uptake.

  • take a toll on + the environment / our health

    Fast fashion takes a heavy toll on the environment.

    3is bad for the earth → 5**takes a heavy toll on the environment

  • irreversible + damage / harm / loss

    Some damage to coral reefs is irreversible.

  • incentive offer / provide + an incentive

    Offering cash incentives for recycling would change habits faster than posters.

  • green space create / protect + green space

    Green spaces in crowded districts offer relief from both heat and stress.

  • tackle + climate change / pollution / a problem

    No single household can tackle climate change alone, but sensible policies can.

See them at work

Hong Kong cannot tackle climate change with posters alone. Most people already know that single-use plastics take a toll on the environment; what they lack is a reason to change. Offering incentives for returning containers, and a small charge for taking new ones, would curb waste and divert it from landfills, and the habit, once formed, would make a sustainable lifestyle feel normal rather than noble.

4 Health & well-being

  • sedentary + lifestyle / habits / job

    Long hours at a desk have made a sedentary lifestyle normal for students.

    3don't exercise much → 5**lead a sedentary lifestyle

  • prioritise + mental well-being / sleep / health

    Schools should prioritise mental well-being as seriously as grades.

  • stigma the stigma + surrounding mental illness

    The stigma surrounding mental illness stops many teenagers from asking for help.

  • seek help seek + professional help / support

    Seeking professional help is a sign of strength, not weakness.

  • cope with + stress / pressure / loss

    Exercise is one of the healthiest ways to cope with stress.

  • sleep-deprived + students / workers

    A sleep-deprived student may be in the classroom without really learning.

  • well-balanced + diet / meal

    A well-balanced diet does more for concentration than any energy drink.

  • take its toll on + health / the body / the mind

    Years of overtime take their toll on workers' health.

  • preventive + measures / care / check-ups

    Preventive care, such as regular check-ups, costs far less than treatment.

  • chronic + stress / illness / pain

    Chronic stress weakens the immune system.

  • vulnerable to + heatstroke / infection / pressure

    Elderly people living alone are especially vulnerable to heatstroke in summer.

  • resilience build / develop + resilience

    Facing small failures helps teenagers build resilience.

    3become stronger → 5**build resilience

  • work-life balance achieve / strike + a work-life balance

    Long working hours make a healthy work-life balance a distant dream for many employees.

  • physical activity regular / engage in + physical activity

    Regular physical activity lowers the risk of heart disease.

  • in moderation eat / drink / use + in moderation

    Bubble tea is fine in moderation; the problem is one cup every day.

See them at work

For many S5 students, poor health is not a crisis but a habit. A sedentary lifestyle, late nights over homework and a diet of instant noodles slowly take their toll on the body, while chronic stress goes unnoticed because everyone around them is just as tired. Schools that prioritise mental well-being, and teach students to seek help early, would do more than prevent illness: they would help a generation build resilience before it is tested.

5 Youth & social issues

  • disillusioned + with work / politics / the system

    Graduates who cannot afford a flat may become disillusioned with hard work itself.

  • upward mobility limit / offer + upward mobility

    Expensive housing limits upward mobility for young people from poorer families.

  • subdivided flats live in + subdivided flats

    Children living in subdivided flats often have no table to do their homework on.

  • the wealth gap widen / narrow + the wealth gap

    Unequal access to tutoring widens the wealth gap in education.

    3rich and poor are different → 5**the wealth gap widens

  • the generation gap bridge + the generation gap

    Projects that pair students with elderly volunteers help bridge the generation gap.

  • marginalised feel / be + marginalised

    Ethnic minority students can feel marginalised when school life happens only in Chinese.

  • social cohesion strengthen / weaken + social cohesion

    Shared public spaces strengthen social cohesion in a crowded city.

  • voice + concerns / opinions

    Student councils give young people a channel to voice their concerns.

  • give back to + society / the community

    Volunteering lets teenagers give back to the community that raised them.

  • an ageing population cope with / support + an ageing population

    An ageing population will place heavy demands on our healthcare system.

  • stereotype reinforce / challenge + a stereotype

    Calling every teenager lazy reinforces an unfair stereotype.

    3people think teenagers are lazy → 5**a stereotype that teenagers are lazy

  • empower + young people to make choices

    Paid internships empower young people to make informed career choices.

  • combat + loneliness / poverty / discrimination

    Community centres help combat loneliness among elderly people living alone.

  • inclusive + society / design / education

    An inclusive society designs for wheelchair users from the start, not as an afterthought.

  • civic responsibility a sense of + civic responsibility

    Volunteering builds a sense of civic responsibility that no lesson can teach.

See them at work

It is easy to call young people apathetic. It is harder to ask why. A graduate sharing a subdivided flat with her parents, watching the wealth gap widen each year, can hardly be blamed for becoming disillusioned. If we want young people to give back to the community, we must first empower them to voice their concerns, and listen when they do; otherwise we simply reinforce the stereotype we claim to dislike.

6 Hong Kong culture & tourism

  • heritage preserve / protect + heritage

    Preserving heritage is not about freezing the past but keeping it in use.

  • revitalise + old buildings / a district

    Revitalising old buildings can bring visitors to forgotten districts.

    3make old places new → 5**revitalise historic buildings

  • authentic + experience / local culture

    Visitors increasingly want an authentic local experience, not just another mall.

  • a melting pot be + a melting pot of cultures

    Hong Kong has long been a melting pot of Chinese and Western cultures.

  • dwindling + trades / numbers / audiences

    Traditional crafts such as neon-sign making are dwindling trades.

  • livelihood threaten / lose + a livelihood

    Rising rents threaten the livelihoods of small family-run shops.

  • iconic + landmark / skyline / dish

    The Star Ferry is as iconic as the skyline it crosses.

  • boost + tourism / the economy / spending

    Mega events can boost tourism, but only if visitors want to come back.

    3help the economy → 5**boost tourism and local spending

  • cater to + tourists / residents

    Streets of shops that cater only to tourists leave little for residents.

  • evoke + nostalgia / memories

    Old-style cha chaan tengs evoke nostalgia in older customers and curiosity in younger ones.

  • showcase + local talent / culture

    Street festivals showcase local talent that rarely reaches mainstream media.

  • a hidden gem discover / be + a hidden gem

    Small heritage trails can be a hidden gem for visitors tired of shopping.

  • word of mouth spread by / rely on + word of mouth

    Small restaurants rely on word of mouth more than advertising.

See them at work

Tourists do not cross the world to visit a shopping mall they could find at home. What they remember is the authentic Hong Kong: a cha chaan teng that evokes nostalgia, a street of dwindling trades, a hidden gem they heard about by word of mouth. If we want to boost tourism, we should revitalise these places and protect the livelihoods of the people in them, before rising rents replace them with the very shops visitors came here to escape.

7 Work & careers

  • job security offer / lack + job security

    Contract jobs offer flexibility but little job security.

  • career prospects promising / limited + career prospects

    Graduates weigh salary against long-term career prospects.

  • transferable skills develop / gain + transferable skills

    Part-time jobs help students develop transferable skills such as time management.

    3useful skills → 5**transferable skills

  • work ethic a strong + work ethic

    Employers value a strong work ethic as much as qualifications.

  • hands-on experience gain + hands-on experience

    Internships offer hands-on experience that no lecture can.

  • lead to burnout overwork / unpaid overtime + leads to burnout

    Months of unpaid overtime can lead to burnout.

  • work remotely allow staff to + work remotely

    Working remotely saves commuting time but blurs the line between home and office.

  • flexible working hours offer / introduce + flexible working hours

    Flexible working hours help parents balance work and family.

  • climb the career ladder

    Many young people no longer see climbing the career ladder as the only measure of success.

  • productivity boost / hinder + productivity

    Endless meetings hinder productivity more than any distraction.

    3make people work slower → 5**hinder productivity

  • accountable hold someone + accountable for

    Naming one owner for each task holds someone accountable for it.

  • entrepreneurial + spirit / mindset

    Start-up competitions encourage an entrepreneurial spirit among students.

  • job satisfaction find / derive + job satisfaction

    Many people find job satisfaction in meaningful work rather than a higher salary.

See them at work

For many young Hong Kong workers, success no longer means climbing the career ladder at any cost. They have watched parents trade evenings for job security and still burn out. Employers who want to keep them should offer flexible working hours, give real responsibility and let staff gain hands-on experience across teams; a workforce that finds job satisfaction stays longer and works better than one held together by fear.
